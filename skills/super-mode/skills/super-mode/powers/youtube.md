# Power: YouTube

For running a YouTube channel: long videos and Shorts, scripts, titles and thumbnails, retention, SEO, chapters.

## Builds on
The **video-studio** power for the edit, the **writer** power for words, the **social** power for research.
Ideas from youtube-agent-skill (MIT, github.com/Jakeschincariol/youtube-agent-skill); no text copied.

## Method
1. **Find what works now.** Helpers pull the niche's top videos and Shorts of the last 90 days; rank each by
   how far it beat its own channel's median, not by raw views. Name the hook and the packaging it used.
2. **Package before you film.** Title and thumbnail are one idea: write 3 pairs, check the title fits on a
   phone (about 50 to 60 characters), never repeat words between title and thumbnail text, no vague words.
3. **Script.** Write 5 hook options from proven patterns (a result, a question, a contrast, a mistake, a
   number), score them for clarity and curiosity, open with the best, and put a re-hook every 30 to 60 s.
4. **Edit list from the transcript:** dead air, fillers, retakes and slow parts with timestamps, then the
   video-studio power cuts them.
5. **Retention.** Read the retention graph as the user exports it: the drop in the first 30 s (hook leak),
   cliffs (one moment lost people), slow slides (pacing). Give one fix per problem, with its timestamp.
6. **Shorts** from the long video: moments that stand alone, a new first line written for each.
7. **SEO and chapters:** a description that says what the viewer gets in the first two lines, real search
   phrases, chapters from the transcript that follow YouTube's rules (first at 0:00, at least 3, each at
   least 10 s).
8. **Plan the week** around the hours the user really has: one anchor video, a few Shorts.

## Never
Upload, publish or reply for the user. Buy views or fake engagement. Reuse other creators' footage without
the right to.

## Done when
Packaging scored, script hooked, edit list timestamped, every claim about the niche sourced and dated.
